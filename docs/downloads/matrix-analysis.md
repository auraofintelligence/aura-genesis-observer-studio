# Reading the Aura workbooks as spatial cognitive architecture

Research date: 10 November 2026. These are source interpretations for the Genesis Observer Studio, not a replacement specification for the author's developing architecture. The original workbooks were read without modification.

The three workbooks combine address grids, lexicons, boxed process models, resource and time budgets, independent parallel lists, narrative prompts, technical references and provisional ideas. They should not become one flattened questionnaire. A useful Minimum Viable Self preserves this variety: episodes and evidence, language and meaning, values and motives, contextual preferences, action expectations, outcomes, relationships, temporal rhythms and user-chosen spatial associations.

OOXML inspection retained every stored cell, cell coordinate, style reference, formula record and cached value; row/column dimensions; merges; fonts, fills and borders; drawing anchors; hyperlinks; conditional-formatting and data-validation definitions. Embedded images were visually reviewed. The selected memory/action region was reconstructed and visually reviewed with its positions, merges, fills, borders and blank spaces retained. The reconstruction adjusts font metrics and row heights for readability; it is not a pixel-identical Excel screenshot. No numerical model was recalculated and no original file was exported or edited.

## Interpretation rules

1. Read explicit instructions, titles, merge spans and cell boundaries before choosing a reading direction.
2. Treat a boxed region as a candidate conceptual unit, then inspect its internal labels. A border alone does not establish causation.
3. Preserve coordinate and ordering evidence for lists, but do not turn two neighbouring lists into paired records.
4. Separate address identity from content meaning. The same concept can have several locations or lenses; reusing a template does not create another chakra.
5. Distinguish source wording, structural observation, interpretation and proposed extension in every mapping.
6. Treat collections as inventories of candidate concepts until the author assigns relationships. Do not silently convert philosophical associations into measured attributes.
7. Keep contradictory, unfinished and repeated regions visible. Record alternatives instead of resolving them by guesswork.

Topology confidence below means confidence in the visible arrangement, not confidence that a cognitive or psychological theory is scientifically established. **Explicit** means labels or instructions state the relationship. **Structured** means borders, merges and nearby labels support a grouping. **Provisional** means an interpretive connection still needs author confirmation.

## Matrix Mapping

### Address grid and repeated banks

`A1:A16` explicitly describes seven coloured 12 by 24 matrices, horn-torus faces, questions assigned to boxes, and an identity theme in the first column. The top seven address grids are `B3:Y14`, `AB3:AY14`, `BB3:BY14`, `CB3:CY14`, `DB3:DY14`, `EB3:EY14` and `FB3:FY14`. This is 288 facet addresses per colour and 2,016 across seven colours. It does not mean 2,016 answers are required to establish an MVS.

The grid template reappears at rows `308:319`, `612:623`, `916:927` and `1221:1232`. These are five banks of the seven-colour template, with different collections beneath them. They are not evidence for 35 separate chakras or 35 new observer identities.

`A1233` explicitly instructs the reader to switch the reading direction for alternate meaning. `A338` asks whether character traits should be placed in vector colour groups instead of facet matrices. Both matter: the source intentionally allows more than one semantic arrangement and contains an unresolved placement decision. The new system should retain `reading_direction`, `group_id`, `relationship_type`, `source_range` and `mapping_status`, rather than imposing a single row-major interpretation.

### Vocabulary is a material for meaning

`M16:EM16` labels six successive sections of commonly used children's words. The principal vertical word lists start at `N17`, `AN17`, `BN17`, `CN17`, `DN17` and `EN17`; the violet area beginning `FM16` contains grammar-related terms distributed across several columns. This is a lexicon collection, not a claim that the word at the same row across all colours forms one sentence, trait or question.

The heading gives a claimed common-word corpus size and context, but corpus provenance and frequency validation were not independently established here. A questionnaire should ask a participant what a word means to them, for a concrete episode, an exception and an alternative phrasing. It should not infer personality from the presence or position of a word in the source list.

### Traits, practical life and relationship themes

`C322:C333` holds twelve character themes; `N322:N345` contains a colour label, trait words and a second group of semantic lenses including definition, context, synonyms, antonyms, symbolism, examples, ideals, goals, timing and indicators. These are related inventories, but a one-to-one row join would be unjustified.

`AC322:AC333` contains practical life headings such as home, food, communication, learning, transport, travel, parenting and giving. `AN322:AN325` starts a more detailed home list. `BC322:BC333` explores love, courting, receiving love, responsible exit, negotiation, family planning and later chapters. `CC322:CC324` is a short environment/business/enjoyment collection. These regions invite branching questions and examples, rather than assigning every answer to a fixed colour permanently.

`N626` begins an emotions collection; `AC626:AC628` contains learning, travel and creative goals; `BC626:BC628` names family, courting and friends. Adjacent repeated words remain source material, not automatically completed questions. The heading “All Questions Sequentially” is an intention for some columns; it does not turn every word in those columns into an authored question.

### Time is an addressable context

`M929:Q929` labels a clock framework; `N930:N953` runs through very large and very small temporal scales. `AA945:AJ955` contains first activation, activation count, latest activation, timeline/calendar, renewal, downtime, thresholds, reminders, synchronisation and backup. This supports timestamps, intervals, review cycles and event histories in MVS records. The list also mixes scientific labels and conceptual scales; for example “light years” appears among time labels, so these entries must not become an unquestioned unit-conversion engine.

### Multiple abstract systems coexist

The final bank begins with high-priority focal points (`C1235:C1250`, extending further), a detailed interface and personal-concept list (`N1235:N1250`, extending further), an original keyword sequence (`AC1235`, `AN1235:AN1250`, extending further), a character-code table (`BM1234:BP1490`), interrogatives and action words (`CN1235:CN1250`, extending further), startup ideas (`DM1234:DN1250`, extending further) and self/practice headings (`EC1235:EC1250`, extending further).

The heading calls the code table “256 Bit ASCii Code”; this wording is retained as source text, not endorsed as a technical definition. A character-code reference, a startup inventory and a self-model prompt bank should be separate typed collections that may be linked deliberately. Adjacency is not evidence that a character code causes a value, startup or personal trait.

## LifeLogging Part 1

### Governance and action come before the profile

`B2:K8` places privacy, independence, interdependence, information flow, governance and best practices together. `B10:D17` distinguishes project-, item-, place-, person-, emotion-, game- and time-specific action development. `B19:J25` separates importance, urgency, value, rewards, information sequence and foresight/hindsight. `B27:N37` then adds needs/wants, time, experience, ambition, associations, tools, interests, limitations and rewards.

This supports distinct fields for importance, urgency, desired value, evidence, available resources and constraints. Combining them into one “priority” or “personality” score would discard meaning. A permission field records a person's decision; it is not an access-control mechanism on its own.

### Hyper-busy is a source mode, with no supplied multiplier

`B51` reads “Time: ratio for proces re-ordering”. `B52:B62` lists Casual; Casual → Busy; Casual → Hyper Busy; Part Time → Casual/Busy/Hyper Busy; Full Time → Casual/Busy/Hyper Busy; Immersion → Busy/Hyper Busy.

There are no numerical ratios or daily-duration rules beside those labels. `F51:F62` is an independent material-properties list, while `J51:J60` contains intelligence-quotient notes. They are not matching mode parameters.

An implementation can therefore adapt the sequence and granularity of reflection to available time, interruption and attention while preserving ambition and the total learning objectives. The specific schedule, session length or multiplier chosen for the 120-hour protocol must be labelled as a new design decision rather than quoted as source instruction. “Hyper-busy” should not imply measured thought throughput or a required state of arousal.

### Boxed cognitive models

`B66:H75` combines memory, perception, action selection, action, arousal, a selection threshold, motivation and urges/drives. `B79:H93` elaborates protocol memory, long-term memory, the present, comparison with expectation, an active motive, activation, demand sensors and named needs.

`B97:H109` puts long-term memory, world model(s) and a mental stage above perception, action selection and action. A motive-selection region sits beneath them. Needs/values/virtues/beliefs/attitudes/desires/unknowns, emotions and interests occupy separate bands. `C113:H136` adds competing options, self model, declarative and procedural memory, frame, plans and expectation, followed by motive selection and the same value/emotion/interest bands.

The borders and merged spans in the [SVG reconstruction](matrix-spatial-preview.svg) support reading this as related architecture sketches. They do not define an executable graph or establish which direction every edge runs. The source's later action-circuit sentence supplies stronger evidence for a loop: `C173` describes cycles of observations, questions and actions producing multiple possible pathways towards goals in good conscience.

`A135:H136` adds two separate parameter collections: motivators (weight, gain/creation, loss/decay, baseline, volatility, range, duration) and modulators (valence, arousal, confrontation, understanding, focus, detail and direction of attention). A new model can collect these as self-reported, contextual annotations, with units and meanings chosen by the person. It should not treat them as an established scoring instrument.

### Context, place and participation

`B172:G200` combines action cycles, rational filters, symbolic encoding, conscience, celebration, art/science, friendship, cultural context, power/respect and mastery. `B202:I221` repeats a useful lens across doing, sharing and creating: what, with whom, where, when, why consider it and why not. `B233:B256` adds situation, relevant events and place-dependent conditions. `B294:B304` provides a concrete activity example: location, schedule, transport, regional options, conditions, arrival timing and a final instruction request.

These support an ambitious agent architecture with context retrieval, candidate actions, opposing reasons, decisions and later outcomes. They are not a licence for an agent to act merely because it can imagine a beneficial outcome. The record should specify which actions it may prepare, which require a person to choose, and who bears the consequences.

### Recollection, relationships and evidence

`B479:B518` includes a relationship reflection sequence and an instruction to answer again to remain synchronised with current feelings. `B522:B624` is a broad list-making collection. These include borrowed-looking question formats and links; the new questionnaire should use original phrasing and preserve attribution when a specific borrowed exercise is reused.

`B630:B647` is a compact narrative vocabulary: motives, peaks, pain, emotions, firsts, joy, challenge, gifts, empathy, choice, hope, belief, values, attitude, happiness, tools and models. Beside it, `L631:T631` lists cause, effect, resulting state, delay, reason for delay, evidence of state/work/reputation and point-of-view summary. The horizontal evidence headings are an independent schema, not nine answers for the “Motivations” row.

### December 2026 simulation anchor

`B41` says “My Age at December 3rd”; `K41` is 44 and `K42` is 2026. This directly corroborates the requested age-44 December 2026 framing. It does not establish what will have happened by that date. Prefer “December 2026, after the birthday” for the scenario, and label all prospective answers as simulated rather than memories or testimony.

## LifeLogging Part 2

This sheet strongly demonstrates why row-wise parsing fails. `A1` says to apply time to a horn torus. `A2:C140` is a time-measure reference collection. `D2:D43` separately collects thought/communication/urge throughput ideas and states. `E2:E42` collects personal, relational, historical and environmental context. `F2:J140` contains different life-stage rehearsal lists, `K2:K140` degree titles, and `L2:L30` an unevenly titled collection including language, embodiment, navigation, agency, goals, decision-making, tool creation, teamwork and resources. The title “Parenthood” above column L does not explain all these technical entries.

`N1:X19` contains schooling/time-allocation scenarios; `Z1:AK11` has conversion-like values and heartbeat/breath candidates; `AM1:AO3` compares basic, intermediate and expert torus capacities. These are separate neighbourhoods. The overlaid learning-area table spans approximately `L3:O34`; it supplies another visual reference with its own categories, not cell data to join to column L.

The sheet provides no formulas. Numeric candidates therefore remain supplied values, not validated calculations. For example, `T2:X2` contains five percentages whose sum is 135. It is not clear whether these modes are simultaneous, illustrative or meant as exclusive categories. Values for thoughts, typing, reading, speech, urges, heartbeats and breaths must not become normative targets or personal predictions. A scheduling prototype should use actual session time and participant-reported availability instead.

## Implications for the Minimum Viable Self

The source supports a **small trusted core with an extensive expandable collection**, rather than a shallow profile or a claim to capture the entire person. “Minimum viable” should refer to enough reviewed context to perform a chosen task well; density belongs in the optional layers and links.

Recommended record fields:

| Collection | What to capture | Relevant source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and interface | Chosen names/roles, present chapter, communication/access preferences, language, what the system must ask rather than assume | Matrix practical themes; Matrix Studio Know Thy Self |
| Permission and agency | Intended audience, use permissions, action boundaries, expiry/review, correction and withdrawal requests | Part 1 B2:K8, B27:N37 |
| Narrative episodes | Context, event, participant wording, meaning then/now, source, uncertainty and relevance | Part 1 B630:T647 |
| Values and tensions | Meaning, episode, counterexample, conflict, trade-off and chosen next action | Part 1 B105:H109; Matrix N334:N345 |
| Embodied circumstances | Self-described energy, comfort, accessibility and conditions affecting available action | Part 1 A135:H136; Part 2 E2:E42 |
| Relationships and participation | Relationship terms, reciprocity, shared activity, boundaries, commitments and differences | Part 1 B202:I221; Matrix BC322:BC333 |
| Skills and procedural knowledge | Demonstrable task, example, tools, limits, teach-back, next practice | Part 1 D116:E117; Part 2 L3:L30 |
| Goals and resources | Horizon, importance, urgency, desired value, constraints, available resources, alternatives | Part 1 B19:N37; Matrix AC626:AC628 |
| World models and uncertainty | Belief, basis, competing explanation, confidence in own words and disconfirming observation | Part 1 F97:H103, H105 |
| Action and learning | Trigger, options, chosen action, expectation, observed result, delay, revision | Part 1 B79:H93, C113:H123, L631:T631 |
| Timing and review | Date/approximation, recurrence, last activation, next review, expiry and workload mode | Matrix N930:AJ955; Part 1 B51:B62 |
| Meaning and aesthetics | Favourite language, symbols, stories, art, rhythms and personally chosen associations | Matrix lexicons, Part 1 B168:G168 and B175:F182 |

Each answer should have a stable record ID; question ID; author/observer role; context; event time and capture time; evidence/source IDs; claim kind (`self-report`, `observation`, `interpretation`, `proposal`, `simulation`); uncertainty; intended audience and permitted uses; review date; and optional one-to-many spatial addresses. Preserve “not answered”, “unknown”, “not applicable”, “declined” and “contradictory” as different states.

The core response should remain the person's wording. Derived tags and summaries are separate, attributable records that can be rejected without deleting the original. Three observers can compare the same event without overwriting each other: preserve each observation and its provenance, then keep a separate reviewed synthesis. The workbooks support multiple perspectives and expectation comparison; they do not, by themselves, specify the complete three-observer roles. Use the Genesis source documents for that definition.

### Dense question patterns derived from the architecture

These are new prompts inspired by the source, not copied questionnaire text:

1. What do you mean by this value, when did you enact it, and when did a competing value win?
2. Which familiar word means something unusual to you, and what example would prevent an assistant misunderstanding it?
3. What changed between your remembered event, your interpretation then and your interpretation today?
4. What would a nearby observer have seen, and which part of your experience would remain invisible to them?
5. What expectation did you form before acting, what happened, and what would you now revise?
6. Which option did you reject, why, and what change in circumstances would make it worth reconsidering?
7. Which recurring condition changes your priorities without changing your values?
8. What is urgent but unimportant, important but not urgent, and valuable despite neither?
9. What can the Aura prepare independently, and where must it return the decision to you?
10. What should it remember for this task, what should it forget, and what must never be inferred from neighbouring records?
11. What do you know how to do that is easier to demonstrate than describe?
12. What would count as evidence that you had taught that skill successfully?
13. Which belief helps you act, what is its evidence, and what observation would lead you to change it?
14. What is the strongest opposing interpretation of a story you tell about yourself?
15. When a plan stalls, is the delay due to resources, timing, dependency, reluctance or an incorrect model?
16. What does reciprocity mean in this relationship, and how would the other person describe it differently?
17. What would a respectful exit from this commitment require?
18. What can you do, share or create with someone; where, when, and for what reciprocal benefit?
19. Which kinds of interruption should change the order of your next actions, and which should be parked?
20. What is the smallest observation you can capture during a hyper-busy day that makes later reflection accurate?
21. Which ambitious project becomes practical if a resource constraint is removed, and which still lacks a clear purpose?
22. Which symbol or spatial arrangement helps you remember something, and what alternative arrangement changes its meaning?
23. Which account of yourself is outdated, which is still useful, and which is a hopeful future simulation?
24. What evidence would justify promoting a simulated answer into a reviewed personal record?

Each question can expand through time, place, relationship, exception, counterexample, resource, consequence and review lenses. Density should produce distinctions and usable evidence; repeating the same prompt across 2,016 addresses adds volume without necessarily adding meaning.

## Relationship to Aura Matrix Studio

The local Matrix Studio README explicitly describes MVS collection before Genesis and eventual personal nanoLLM training. It says model training and sustained Genesis conversations are not implemented. Its fixed 12 by 24 geometry, dataset catalogue, editable chakra associations, separate interior/exterior addresses and local collection tools are relevant, but the README labels its route as evolving design configuration.

The dataset catalogue supplies 37 recommended datasets and seven complementary philosophical perspectives. It supports one-to-many associations and explains that inside/outside is not access control. Its current projects and import routes can inspire an exchange schema; they do not prove the new questionnaire is already integrated. Import/export should use a reviewed, versioned adapter and preserve unknown fields. Do not imply automatic nanoLLM training or clinical assessment from completing this collection.

## Open questions and coverage limits

- Which of the five matrix banks are alternatives, successive stages or simultaneous collections? The repeated template establishes layout, not the intended relationship.
- Which trait allocations belong on facets, vectors or both? The author explicitly leaves this open at Matrix `A338`.
- Which reading directions produce which relationships? The reversal instruction is explicit, but no complete rule set is supplied.
- How should linguistic lists, narrative episodes and action models cross-link? New links must retain their author and evidence.
- Are the numerical throughput and school-allocation values assumptions, illustrations or targets? No normative meaning should be assigned without clarification.
- What sources and reuse terms apply to the embedded pictures and borrowed-looking prompt collections? They remain local pending attribution.
- Counts cover all stored cells, but semantic interpretation focuses on named structural regions; no claim is made that every one of the 30,105 nonempty stored values has been exhaustively interpreted.
- These sources do not establish that a questionnaire produces consciousness, a digital continuation of a person, accurate future predictions or a clinically valid assessment. They establish a substantial design vocabulary for a personal knowledge and reflection system.

The ambitious next step is a working cycle: capture a meaningful episode, preserve its contexts and alternative views, choose an action, observe the result, and revise the model while retaining the history. The 120-hour protocol can repeatedly exercise that cycle at personal, interpersonal, project, community and wider-world scales.
