Source catalogue

Weaving a hundred thousand useful Aura questions

This specification turns the authored seed questions and source maps into a large, navigable question atlas. The target is of the order of 100,000 useful candidate questions, developed through bounded batches, evidence checks, semantic review and continuing correction. The atlas is a resource to move through selectively. It is not a requirement that a person answer 100,000 questions, or that the entire collection be traversed during 120 Genesis hours.

The central task is to discover worthwhile distinctions, missing perspectives, testable alternatives and useful next actions. Changing a few words in a question does not create another worthwhile question. A large count without a coverage map, redundancy control and review history is not the intended result.

What a lighter agent receives

Give each generator one territory packet, not the whole personal archive. A territory is a defined field of inquiry inside or across the 24 broad domains. The current catalogue has 100 territories: 60 whole-of-life, 30 teacher-development and 10 Aura-architecture territories. Territory IDs and their boundaries come from that catalogue. Do not invent IDs or force every territory to produce an equal quota.

Each packet contains:

  1. A territory ID, purpose and boundary: what understanding or action this part of the atlas should enable, and which neighbouring territories own related work.
  2. Exact source references: source ID, hash or version, page/section or sheet/range, the relevant short excerpt or structural observation, and the rights/access boundary for using it.
  3. Source interpretation status: explicit source wording, structured spatial grouping or provisional interpretation. Include unresolved alternatives rather than hiding them.
  4. A coverage gap: the specific missing construct, context, operation or relationship the batch should address. “More questions” is not an adequate gap.
  5. Five to fifteen relevant seed questions and the closest already accepted questions, including their semantic signatures and reasons for keeping them.
  6. Permitted variation: episode, relationship, condition, self lens, information available, resource constraint, consequence, action or evaluation method that can change the knowledge elicited.
  7. A scenario boundary: use a fictional or generic example unless the participant supplied and permitted a real one. Never invent the person's experience to make a question feel personalised.
  8. A batch contract: output IDs reserved by the coordinator, 25 or 50 candidate slots, format, budget, review owner and stop condition.

Use public and source-design context where it is sufficient. A generator does not need private answers merely to create general questions. A later interview navigator can use permissioned answers to choose among accepted questions or draft a clearly labelled contextual follow-up.

These are actionable starting gaps, not an assertion that all ranges have now been fully interpreted. Each generation packet should narrow a broad range to the actual relevant cells or source passage before a question is accepted as source-grounded. A question may be a new design inference inspired by a source; label it that way.

Coverage before volume

Maintain a coverage ledger for each territory. It records the source constructs represented, question operations used, contexts explored, missing perspectives, known near-duplicates and unresolved source ambiguities. Broad domain counts alone cannot show whether the architecture is covered.

Useful operations include describing an episode, defining a term, reconstructing a sequence, comparing conditions, distinguishing observation from inference, predicting an outcome, seeking counterevidence, designing an intervention, repairing a misunderstanding, allocating resources, teaching a concept, testing transfer and reviewing an actual result. A territory should not become hundreds of definitions when its purpose requires action and feedback.

Useful variation changes the answer that could be given. For example, a question about noticing an alarm while alone differs from one about distributing attention when another steward has agreed responsibility, because the available information and social decision structure change. Asking the same question with “workshop”, “studio” and “room” substituted does not necessarily add meaning.

For each proposed question, require a brief novelty reason: what new evidence, distinction or decision could the answer add compared with the nearest existing question? If the generator cannot answer that, it should return fewer candidates or a gap note instead of padding the batch.

A planning distribution of 100 territories × 1,000 questions can make the target visible, but it is not an acceptance rule. Reallocate capacity when one territory is saturated and another has meaningful gaps. Keep generated candidates, accepted unique questions and human-sampled questions as separate counts. Never present 100,000 attempted or mechanically combined strings as 100,000 individually reviewed questions.

The three-agent production contract

Generator

The generator proposes 25 questions per ordinary batch. Use 50 only for a well-understood territory with enough distinct gaps and stable source interpretation. It works from the packet, writes questions and records its source/design reasoning. It does not answer the questions, invent participant facts, approve itself or expand its tool permissions. If source semantics are unresolved, it can create a question about the ambiguity or explain the gap in generationNotes; it cannot resolve the ambiguity silently. The current runtime packet supplies curated source summaries and locators, not complete primary-source passages. The worker must say which summary informed its design and what it did not inspect.

Critic

The critic receives the same packet, all candidates and nearby accepted questions. It checks evidence, conceptual distinctness, clarity, answerability, coercive assumptions and consequence. It reports specific question IDs and exact defects. It must identify at least one plausible failure mode or explicitly state that it found none after named checks; it must not manufacture criticism to fill a quota. A different model or prompt context can provide another view, but does not make the critic an independent real-world witness.

Reconciler

The reconciler accepts supported critique, repairs only the affected candidates and preserves disagreement. It does not rewrite a good question merely to sound different. It records which version changed, why and whether the change affected its semantic signature. It can merge duplicates into one question with several routes, defer an item for author interpretation, or reject it. The coordinator performs final schema/source/duplicate checks after reconciliation.

Use a maximum of two revision cycles per candidate. After that, retain a useful unresolved note or reject the candidate and move on. Escalate only the specific semantic or authority question requiring a human decision. A difficult territory is not solved by unbounded retries or by lowering its standards to reach the total.

Prompt contracts

These instructions are intended for the lower-cost generation workers. Tool and model selection is recorded by the coordinator; no particular current vendor/model is assumed by this document.

Generator instruction

Produce up to the requested 25 or 50 new Aura reflection questions for this territory packet. Use only supplied source identities and loci. Questions are newly authored design work, not quotations. Each must add a substantive distinction relative to the supplied nearest questions. Preserve source ambiguities. Ask for evidence or meaning without claiming the participant has an experience, trait or condition. Do not generate answers. Return only JSON matching the candidate schema. If a slot would add only paraphrase, omit it and record the uncovered gap. Stay inside the packet's tool, input and output budgets.

Critic instruction

Inspect every candidate against the packet and rubric. For each issue, cite the question ID, the problematic wording, the source or nearest-question comparison, and a concrete repair or rejection reason. Check whether changed context changes the knowledge elicited. Do not infer private facts. Do not treat a source list's spatial proximity as a semantic join. Return only the review schema. Distinguish hard failures, repairable quality gaps and optional improvements. Do not approve any candidate whose source locus is absent or whose novelty depends only on paraphrase.

Reconciler instruction

Reconcile the candidate and critique without changing the territory's purpose. Preserve valid source boundaries and the participant's right to decline. Return revised candidates only where needed, with parent IDs and change reasons, plus dispositions for every reviewed candidate. Merge semantic duplicates instead of rephrasing them. Do not create answers, new source claims or human-review status. After two revision cycles, defer or reject unresolved candidates. Return only the reconciliation schema.

Strict candidate JSON

Use the exact schema returned by runtime/weave.py packet for the selected job. It supplies existing territory/domain IDs, available reserved slots and allowed dimension labels. The generator may not add answer or review-status fields. The implementation accepts partial batches of one to the assigned capacity; if there is no worthwhile candidate, stop and return a coordinator gap report rather than import an empty or fabricated batch.

The top-level keys are exactly schema, jobId, territoryId, domainId, author, questions and generationNotes. The schema value is aura-weaving-batch/1. The author object contains exactly kind (agent or human), name and runId.

Each question contains exactly these fields:

sourceNotes must state the relevant supplied locator, the source/design connection, and what was not inspected or remains uncertain. It must not claim to have read a complete passage when only the registry summary was provided. A reviewer checks that the explanation is supported. The optional personal-disclosure route belongs in the question's wording or encounter instructions; it is not an extra schema key. The novelty reason belongs in gap, with the practical consequence in transfer.

Unknown keys, unresolved placeholders, invented source IDs, non-reserved question IDs and any answer/review/acceptance field fail the strict import contract. A source ID check confirms that the ID is allowed, not that its meaning has been interpreted correctly. Exact-locus and semantic support still require review of the actual packet and relevant source. The current importer rejects exact normalised duplicates and emits near-token-overlap warnings; it does not implement a complete global embedding-based semantic review.

Critic and reconciler records are separate review artefacts, not fields inserted into generator output. A review sidecar should identify jobId, packet version, reviewer run and one disposition per question. Each disposition records the question ID, decision, rubric scores, hard failures, exact issues, nearest duplicate IDs, source checks and reason. A reconciler returns revised strict-schema candidates plus a sidecar with parent version, reason and merge/defer decisions. The import operation creates generated records only. The explicit reviewer operation can set accepted, needs_revision or rejected; a generator cannot grant those states to itself.

The two-cycle limit in this guideline governs deliberate review and revision. The current runtime does not automatically run that loop. A coordinator requests a revision, inspects it and deliberately imports a permitted revision; there is no implied unattended agent service.

Semantic redundancy controls

Run exact normalised text comparison against the whole catalogue, then lexical and semantic nearest-neighbour retrieval. Normalisation may remove case, spacing and punctuation for comparison but must not alter the displayed prompt. Search beyond the same domain; an enterprise decision question may duplicate a resources or agency question.

A semantic signature contains territory, construct, chosen episode/decision, information state, relevant condition, requested operation, evidence needed and intended use. Matching signatures are a strong duplicate warning even if wording differs. Different signatures are not automatic proof of novelty: a reviewer must still ask whether answering both would add meaningful information.

Similarity thresholds are triage aids, not truth. A provisional token-set overlap of 0.82 or an embedding similarity of 0.93 can route a pair for review after the method has been calibrated on the project's labelled duplicate/distinct pairs. Do not discard questions solely on an uncalibrated number. Preserve pairs that ask materially different things, such as predicting before an event and reviewing the actual outcome afterwards. Merge pure paraphrases into one question with alternate wording or several navigation links.

Do not generate a Cartesian product of every trait, colour, self lens and context and call the result semantic coverage. Many combinations are meaningless or redundant. A combination earns a place only when its novelty reason explains the evidence or decision it adds.

Quality gates and sampling

Every candidate must pass automated shape, identity, allowed-source-ID, prohibited-answer-field and exact-duplicate checks. Source-locus meaning and support receive a separate review; the current importer does not verify them merely by accepting a source ID. Every candidate receives a critic disposition. Machine acceptance means it passed the machine process; it does not mean Luke or another human approved it.

For a new territory, manually inspect the first two batches in full before increasing its throughput. Once the territory has a stable interpretation and fewer than 5% substantive defects in those inspected batches, sample at least 10% of accepted questions per later batch, with a minimum of three. Inspect all questions flagged for source ambiguity, sensitive presuppositions, disputed semantics or uncertain duplicate decisions. These rates are an initial quality design, not statistical proof that the unsampled remainder is flawless.

If a sampled hard failure appears, hold the affected batch and inspect it fully. If more than 5% of an inspected sample has substantive repairable defects, return that territory to full inspection for the next two batches and revise its packet. Record the actual sample, reviewer, defects and decisions. Do not label an entire batch “human-reviewed” because three items were sampled.

Track these metrics by territory and source region: candidate count; accepted unique count; rejection and merge rates; exact-source resolution rate; ambiguity rate; distribution of operations and conditions; sampled defect rate; average revision count; useful-gap closure; participant correction rate; and actual use in encounters. A high acceptance rate is not necessarily success if the packet merely generates easy repetitive questions. A high correction rate can reveal an informative gap rather than a deficient participant.

Separate knowledge and personal states

Maintain conceptually separate fields for question generation (seed, generated), editorial review (unreviewed, machine-accepted, human-reviewed, disputed, retired), publication (private-candidate, candidate-catalogue, reviewed-catalogue) and the participant's answer state (unvisited, declined, answered, participant-reviewed, revised). None automatically advances the others. These richer categories are a metadata design: the current ledger implements generated and explicit review states plus answer references; it must not be described as already enforcing every proposed publication field.

A generated question is not a fact about the person. An answered question is not consent to training. A participant-reviewed answer confirms their intended account, not every external claim within it. A model-derived interpretation remains attributed to the model. The three observers retain their separate evidence; the participant can add meaning without the system collapsing those records into one verdict.

Keep source versions and question lineage. If a source interpretation changes, identify questions that depended on it, mark them for review and retain their previous versions. If a participant declines a route, offer a fictional example, a different domain, a practical task or a pause. Do not infer hidden traits from refusal or use the number of answered questions as a ranking of the person.

Interview navigation and hyper mode

The live navigator chooses the next useful question from the current purpose, selected context, unresolved contradiction and available attention. It should be able to explain why it chose that question and what would change the route. It can move sideways, return to an earlier record or end an inquiry. A high-volume atlas makes those routes richer; it does not justify flooding the participant with unrelated questions.

Hyper-mode prepared content is delivered at 260–300 words per minute. A ten-minute stream has a 2,600–3,000-word budget; a fifteen-minute stream has 3,900–4,500 words. This is content throughput, not a target number of self-disclosures per minute. Questions, examples and transitions count when delivered; the participant's thinking, answers, silence and practical work occupy their own actual time. If the stream pauses, the stream clock pauses. Source narration at 130–150 wpm played at 2× reaches the requested range; the target is not doubled again.

An encounter can mix a dense explanatory stream, a carefully selected question, a three-observer scenario, a prototype and review. The atlas should support that whole action circuit. A thousand completed forms are less useful than a smaller set of records that help a person build, teach, collaborate, revise and extend what they can actually do.

Starting production sequence

  1. Validate the source and territory catalogues. Resolve IDs and exact loci; keep unresolved topology in an ambiguity queue.
  2. Pilot five contrasting territories: personal language, observer information states, practical action, a relationship/permission boundary, and a frontier hypothesis. Generate 25 candidates each.
  3. Critique all 125, reconcile at most twice and inspect the pilot in full. Calibrate duplicate decisions using real pairs from this output.
  4. Revise packets and run a second 25-question batch in each territory. Compare defect types, coverage gains and substantive novelty.
  5. Expand to the wider territory catalogue only where the source reading and generation quality hold. Use 50-question batches selectively.
  6. Publish counts by state and a visible coverage/gap map. Continue toward the order-of-100,000 target without implying that ungenerated, rejected or mechanically varied items are accepted questions.
  7. Let actual encounters create the next gap queue: unclear meaning, unexpected consequences, neglected conditions, missing sources and questions the person wanted but could not find.

This is a production contract and review method. It does not claim that 100,000 reviewed questions already exist. The repository's current catalogue and production report supply the actual counts and verification status.
