Minimum Viable Self and ultra-dense reflection atlas

Edition 1 · 10 November 2026. The 288 domain questions cover the 12 × 24 facets on one side of one torus for one self. The 12 reflection lenses can be reused across questions. The 24 first questions are optional starting anchors. Density means depth and connectedness; it is not a demand to disclose everything. Use any entry point, revisit it in another direction, and leave unknown, declined or not applicable answers visible.

How to use the atlas

Choose one intended use. Select relevant anchors, then deepen useful answers with the lenses below. Record time, origin, meaning, access choices and an action or open question. A two-minute capture may later become a long dialogue. A filled questionnaire is not a trained model, and a complete answer count is not a complete person. The system guide defines the MVS acceptance review.

Twelve reflection lenses

  1. episode — Name a concrete episode, its date or approximate period, and the conditions around it.
  2. origin — Identify what is recalled, directly observed, measured, quoted, inferred or imagined.
  3. environment — Describe the wider available scene without claiming to know what was unobserved.
  4. attention — Describe what you actually noticed and what you learned only afterwards.
  5. narration — Describe observable responses and distinguish the narrator’s interpretation from them.
  6. meaning — Explain in your own words what mattered, including any correction to the narration.
  7. tension — Name attraction, repulsion and urge separately; explore a competing motive, contradiction, cost or other person’s perspective.
  8. counterfactual — Change one condition and explore an alternative explanation or action.
  9. resources — State the time, people, tools, access and support available; distinguish these resources from your perceived resourcefulness in using them.
  10. action — Choose a reversible test, an output, an owner and a criterion for useful change.
  11. permission — Choose who may see this and separately whether retrieval, training or publication is allowed.
  12. review — Set a review point and state what would change your mind; unknown and declined are valid.

Chakra themes for practical questions

The seven tori represent the chakras of the etheric Aura. Use each as a perspective on an everyday situation. Keep the wording concrete enough to answer from experience, make a choice or try a small experiment. This example uses one lesson across seven perspectives.

Chakra Practical focus Example question
Red · Root Embodiment, practical needs, place and stability What change to the room, equipment or schedule would make this lesson easier to attend and repeat?
Orange · Sacral Creativity, enjoyment, relationship and change Which format would make this lesson enjoyable to explore, and what quick prototype could you try?
Yellow · Solar plexus Agency, intentions, capability and action What could a learner do unaided by the end, and which task would let them show it?
Green · Heart Care, reciprocity, belonging and stewardship Whose experience would improve this lesson, and what could you offer in return for their input?
Blue · Throat Expression, communication and shared understanding Which explanation would let a new learner describe the idea accurately in their own words?
Indigo · Brow Attention, interpretation, patterns and discernment What observation would help you tell whether a learner understood the idea or remembered the example?
Violet · Crown Meaning, values, integration and wider perspective Which wider purpose does this lesson serve, and what would change if that purpose took priority?

The library gives each seed question a primary theme for browsing and retargeting. The seed still covers one torus, one side and one self. A theme describes what a question asks about. A facet placement specifies where you choose to put it. The themes follow Matrix Studio’s philosophical perspectives.
